# AI Benchmark — August 17

An important experiment was conducted by asking different AI systems about the identity **Marii Cuadros**.

The objective was to observe whether the entity could be retrieved independently without providing extensive conversational context.

---

## ChatGPT Observation

ChatGPT responded that it could not identify a clearly established public figure with the exact name **Marii Cuadros**.

The response suggested that the name might correspond to a creator or social media personality but considered the available public results ambiguous.

---

## Perplexity Observation

Perplexity associated the query with another person named **María Esther Cuadros Espinoza**, a Peruvian public figure.

The system also acknowledged that the query could refer to another person and requested additional context.

This represented an example of **entity ambiguity**.

---

## Gemini Observation

Gemini reported that it did not have sufficient contextual information to identify a public figure named **Marii Cuadros**.

It returned unrelated or generic references associated with the term "MariaCuadros" and artistic or decorative contexts.

---

## Interpretation

The three systems produced substantially different results for the same entity query.

This demonstrated an important research variable:

> Different AI systems can produce different representations of the same name depending on their available information, retrieval mechanisms and internal knowledge.

---

## Methodological Observation

Conversational context can influence AI responses.

For this reason, future entity-recognition tests should distinguish between:

**Contextual conversation**

and

**independent retrieval testing.**

Incognito or otherwise context-reduced testing was therefore adopted as an experimental control where practical.

# Week 2 — Preliminary Findings

Several important observations emerged during the second week.

## Finding 1 — Entity ambiguity exists

Different AI systems initially associated the name **Marii Cuadros** with different entities or could not identify the intended person.

This demonstrated the practical relevance of entity disambiguation as a research problem.

---

## Finding 2 — AI systems behave differently

ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini produced different responses to the same entity query.

This indicates that AI systems should not be treated as a single homogeneous information environment.

---

## Finding 3 — Public indexing changed during the experiment

Between August 13 and August 19, the visibility of the entity changed across several search environments.

The exact causal mechanisms remain undetermined.

---

## Finding 4 — Medium became an observable node

Medium appeared as an identifiable source associated with the entity during later testing.

This created a new variable for future experiments.

---

## Finding 5 — Search and social distribution should be measured separately

Changes in Google or AI retrieval should not automatically be attributed to changes in Instagram, TikTok or other social platforms.

The experiment therefore began treating:

- Search visibility
- AI retrieval
- Social reach
- Engagement
- Platform indexing

as separate measurement categories.

# Week 2 — Research Hypotheses

Based on the observations of this week, the following hypotheses were established for future testing.

### H1 — Distributed Identity

A coherent network of public digital nodes may improve the probability that different sources are associated with the same entity.

### H2 — Source Availability

The appearance of new publicly accessible sources may influence the information available to search and AI systems.

### H3 — Platform Timing

Different platforms may exhibit different indexing and distribution timelines.

### H4 — AI Retrieval Differences

AI systems may differ significantly in their ability to identify and disambiguate newly documented entities.

### H5 — Content Distribution

Changes in social media activity may influence visibility within individual platforms, but this relationship should be measured independently from search-engine indexing.

### H6 — Publication History

The accumulation of public documentation over time may affect the amount and consistency of information available about an entity.

None of these hypotheses were considered proven at the end of Week 2.

# Methodological Rules Established During Week 2

The experiment established several operational rules.

### Rule 1 — Use context-reduced testing

Whenever possible, entity recognition should be tested without previous conversational context.

### Rule 2 — Separate observation from interpretation

A change in visibility is recorded first as an observation.

Possible explanations are documented separately.

### Rule 3 — Do not treat AI responses as primary evidence

AI systems can help discover information and formulate hypotheses, but their explanations about their own systems or external platforms require independent verification.

### Rule 4 — Track platforms independently

Search engines, AI systems and social networks should be treated as separate environments.

### Rule 5 — Preserve historical states

Research documentation should remain available so that changes can be compared over time.
