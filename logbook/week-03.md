# Week 3 Overview

The third week marked a transition from initial indexing experiments toward the systematic organization of the entity and the observation of AI representation.

During this period, AIO CODE focused on:

- Updating the public knowledge infrastructure.
- Improving consistency across GitHub, Blogger and Hugging Face.
- Monitoring independent AI retrieval.
- Observing changes in AI representation after public documentation updates.
- Testing cross-platform content distribution.
- Experimenting with English-language positioning.
- Observing Instagram, Facebook and TikTok behavior.
- Identifying inconsistencies within the public representation of the entity.
- Revising the content strategy.
- Beginning a manual cleanup of the distributed digital identity.

A central methodological question emerged:

> Does increasing the consistency and semantic organization of public information change how AI systems identify and describe the entity?

The experiment remains observational and does not assume causality between individual actions and changes in AI retrieval.

# Week 3 — Preliminary Findings

Several observations emerged during the third week.

## Finding 1 — Entity visibility can change after structural updates

Changes in public documentation and platform structure coincided with changes in AI representation.

The experiment cannot currently determine whether the changes were caused by individual updates or by the accumulation of multiple signals.

---

## Finding 2 — Consistency became a central variable

The experiment identified inconsistencies between different descriptions of:

- Marii Cuadros
- AIO CODE
- VOID MODE

These inconsistencies became a priority for correction.

---

## Finding 3 — More content does not necessarily mean better representation

Increasing the number of public publications did not automatically produce improved AI or social visibility.

The experiment therefore began shifting from:

> More content

toward:

> More coherent information.

---

## Finding 4 — AI systems produce different representations

The differences previously observed between ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity continued into Week 3.

The addition of Grok reinforced the observation that AI systems should be treated as separate environments.

---

## Finding 5 — Social visibility and AI visibility remain separate variables

Instagram, Facebook and TikTok performance did not consistently correspond with changes in AI retrieval.

The experiment therefore continued treating:

- Social distribution
- Search visibility
- AI retrieval
- Entity representation
- Engagement

as separate measurement categories.

---

## Finding 6 — Entity representation can become less stable

After the latest documentation changes, previously observed AI representations changed.

This introduced an important research question:

> Can attempts to improve entity consistency temporarily destabilize the representation before a new stable state is established?

This question requires further testing.

---

## Finding 7 — Discoverability has multiple layers

The TikTok observation demonstrated a useful distinction between:

- Search engine discoverability.
- Username retrieval.
- Platform-native discovery.
- AI entity recognition.

These should not be treated as identical metrics.

# Week 3 — Emerging Research Direction

During this week, the research began moving toward a more specific question.

The initial question was primarily:

> Can a new digital entity become visible to AI systems?

The emerging question is:

> Can the public information surrounding an entity be intentionally structured so that AI systems identify, distinguish and describe that entity more consistently?

This represents a shift from simple indexing toward **AI representation and entity optimization**.

The experiment therefore began examining concepts including:

- Entity consistency.
- Entity disambiguation.
- AI visibility.
- Answer-oriented information.
- Structured documentation.
- Cross-platform semantic consistency.
- Public evidence.
- AI representation accuracy.

These concepts remain research hypotheses and are not yet presented as a finalized methodology.

# Week 3 — Methodological Rules

The following methodological principles were reinforced.

### Rule 1 — Preserve historical states

Previous versions of the documentation should remain accessible whenever possible.

### Rule 2 — Separate interventions from observations

A publication or structural change is an intervention.

A change observed afterward is an observation.

The relationship between them must remain a hypothesis until independently tested.

### Rule 3 — Use multiple AI systems

No individual AI system should be treated as a universal representation of the digital environment.

### Rule 4 — Use context-reduced testing

Entity recognition should be tested without previous conversational context whenever practical.

### Rule 5 — Separate social and AI metrics

Social engagement does not automatically indicate AI visibility.

### Rule 6 — Preserve negative results

Loss of visibility, failed content experiments and unexpected changes are recorded rather than removed.

### Rule 7 — Reduce production friction

The experiment should remain operationally sustainable for a single researcher.

Efficiency is therefore considered part of the experimental design.
