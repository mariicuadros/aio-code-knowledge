# AIO CODE — Research Principles

**Version:** 1.1  
**Status:** Active  
**Updated:** 2026-09-03

## Purpose

These principles govern how AIO CODE classifies evidence, forms claims, conducts experiments, interprets results and communicates research.

### 1. Evidence Determines Claim Strength
A claim must not be stronger than its supporting evidence.

### 2. Observed Is Not Hypothesized
An observed result remains an observation even when its mechanism is unknown.

### 3. Corroboration Increases Confidence
Independent repeated observations may strengthen confidence, but corroboration is not causation.

### 4. Verification Is Claim-Specific
Verification applies only to the specific claim supported by the available evidence.

### 5. Preserve the Baseline
Pre-intervention state should be preserved whenever practical.

### 6. Measure Before Claiming Improvement
Expected improvement is not observed improvement.

### 7. Control Comparability
Comparisons should preserve equivalent system, query, prompt, language, environment and measurement conditions whenever possible.

### 8. Preserve Negative Results
No change, negative change, failure and inconclusive results are valid research outcomes.

### 9. Do Not Fabricate Evidence
Never fabricate observations, measurements, citations, system outputs, corroboration or verification.

### 10. Preserve Historical Research State
Corrections may update the current record but must not erase the historical state.

### 11. Maintain Entity Identity
Canonical entities must remain distinct from competing or ambiguous representations.

```text
MC-001 — Marii Cuadros
AIO-001 — AIO CODE
```

### 12. Separate Identity From Visibility
Identity must be established before visibility is optimized.

### 13. Distributed Sources Require Consistency
Cross-source consistency is a research variable, not proof of recognition or causation.

### 14. AI Outputs Are System Observations
AI responses are observations of system behavior under recorded conditions, not automatically authoritative facts about reality.

### 15. Search Results Are Dynamic Observations
Search results and associations may change with time, environment and system state.

### 16. Recommendation Is a Separate Behavioral Layer
Recommendation must be evaluated separately from indexation, retrieval, resolution, representation and citation.

### 17. Traceability Is Mandatory
Research claims should be traceable to the entity, observation, experiment, measurement and evidence supporting them.

### 18. Uncertainty Must Remain Visible
Unknown is a valid research state.

### 19. Causal Claims Require Additional Evidence

```text
Intervention
     ↓
Observed Change
     ≠
Proven Causation
```

### 20. Research Should Be Reproducible
Document entity, system, prompt/query, conditions, intervention, measurement and evidence whenever practical.

### 21. Research Should Be Responsible
Use legitimate, documented and proportionate methods. Do not rely on fraud, deception, unauthorized access, abuse or fabricated identities/evidence.

### 22. Findings Must Be Proportional to Evidence
Research communication must distinguish observation, corroboration, verification, interpretation, hypothesis, causal conclusion and unknown.

## Core AIO CODE Rule

> **Evidence determines the strength of the claim.**

> **Identity should be established before visibility is optimized.**

> **Measure before claiming improvement.**

> **A measured change does not automatically establish causation.**

> **Unknown is a valid research result.**
