# AIO CODE — Research Principles

## Purpose

This document defines the core research principles governing AIO CODE.

These principles establish how evidence is classified, how claims are formed, how experiments are interpreted, and how research findings are communicated.

---

## Principle 1 — Evidence Determines Claim Strength

The strength of a research claim must correspond to the strength of the available evidence.

```text
Weak evidence
    ↓
Limited claim

Corroborated evidence
    ↓
Stronger claim

Verified evidence
    ↓
Verified finding
````

A claim must not be stronger than the evidence supporting it.

---

## Principle 2 — Observed Is Not the Same as Hypothesized

AIO CODE distinguishes between what a system actually did and what researchers believe may explain that behavior.

```text
Observation
     ≠
Hypothesis
```

An observed result should remain classified as an observation even when its underlying mechanism is unknown.

---

## Principle 3 — Corroboration Increases Confidence

A phenomenon observed independently across multiple measurements, systems, sources, or conditions may receive a higher evidence classification.

However:

```text
Corroboration
     ≠
Causation
```

Multiple observations can establish that a phenomenon is recurring without establishing why it occurs.

---

## Principle 4 — Verification Is Claim-Specific

A finding is verified only in relation to the specific claim being evaluated.

For example:

```text
Verified:
A specific system produced a specific result under defined conditions.

Not automatically verified:
The complete mechanism responsible for that result.
```

Verification must therefore be precise and bounded.

---

## Principle 5 — Preserve the Baseline

Whenever possible, the state of the system before an intervention should be documented.

```text
Baseline
    ↓
Intervention
    ↓
Post-Intervention Measurement
    ↓
Comparison
```

Without a reliable baseline, claims about change should be limited.

---

## Principle 6 — Measure Before Claiming Improvement

AIO CODE does not define an intervention as successful merely because improvement was expected.

Success must be evaluated through measurement.

```text
Expected Improvement
        ≠
Observed Improvement
```

Only measured evidence can establish whether a defined metric changed.

---

## Principle 7 — Control Comparability

Measurements should be compared under equivalent conditions whenever possible.

Relevant conditions include:

* system
* query
* prompt
* language
* environment
* search mode
* measurement procedure
* time

When conditions change, the difference must be documented.

---

## Principle 8 — Preserve Negative Results

An experiment does not become invalid because the expected result did not occur.

AIO CODE preserves:

* no change
* negative change
* unexpected change
* inconsistent change
* failed intervention
* inconclusive results
* insufficient evidence

Unexpected results are part of the research record.

---

## Principle 9 — Do Not Fabricate Evidence

AIO CODE must never fabricate:

* observations
* measurements
* citations
* system outputs
* experimental results
* verification
* corroboration

Expected results must never be recorded as observed results.

---

## Principle 10 — Preserve Historical Research State

Research records should preserve what was known at the time of observation.

When new evidence changes an interpretation, the previous record should remain available.

```text
Historical Observation
        ↓
New Evidence
        ↓
Updated Interpretation
```

Research evolution should be documented rather than rewritten.

---

## Principle 11 — Maintain Entity Identity

Canonical identity must remain distinct from competing or ambiguous representations.

Example:

```text
Canonical Entity:
MC-001 — Marii Cuadros

Competing Representation:
Maria Luisa Cuadros
```

An incorrect or competing representation must not be silently incorporated into the canonical entity.

---

## Principle 12 — Separate Identity From Visibility

AIO CODE follows the principle:

> Identity should be established before visibility is optimized.

The objective is not simply to increase the amount of information associated with an entity.

The first objective is to establish:

* what the entity is
* what the entity is not
* its canonical name
* its relationships
* its defining attributes
* its authoritative sources

Only after identity is sufficiently established should visibility optimization be evaluated.

---

## Principle 13 — Distributed Sources Require Consistency

A digital entity may exist across multiple information environments.

AIO CODE therefore evaluates consistency across defined sources.

Relevant identity signals may include:

* canonical name
* entity type
* description
* relationships
* identifiers
* structured data
* source relationships

Consistency does not guarantee recognition or retrieval, but inconsistency may complicate interpretation of observed behavior.

---

## Principle 14 — AI Outputs Are System Observations

An AI-generated response is evidence of what that system produced under defined conditions.

It is not automatically an authoritative statement of reality.

The research record should distinguish:

```text
System Output
     ↓
Observed Representation
     ↓
Research Interpretation
```

---

## Principle 15 — Search Results Are Dynamic Observations

Search results, rankings, snippets, associations, recommendations, and retrieved sources may change over time.

Therefore, search observations should be treated as time- and condition-dependent measurements.

A result observed on one date should not automatically be assumed to represent permanent system behavior.

---

## Principle 16 — Recommendation Is a Separate Behavioral Layer

Recommendation should not be treated as an automatic consequence of indexation, retrieval, or entity recognition.

AIO CODE treats:

```text
Indexation
    ↓
Retrieval
    ↓
Entity Resolution
    ↓
Entity Representation
    ↓
Citation
    ↓
Recommendation
```

as distinct stages that may require separate observation and measurement.

---

## Principle 17 — Traceability Is Mandatory

Research artifacts should remain connected across the AIO CODE architecture.

```text
Entity
   ↓
Observation
   ↓
Experiment
   ↓
Measurement
   ↓
Metric
   ↓
Result
   ↓
Conclusion
```

Every experimental conclusion should be traceable to the evidence supporting it.

---

## Principle 18 — Uncertainty Must Remain Visible

When evidence is insufficient, uncertainty should not be hidden.

Appropriate classifications include:

```text
Observed
Corroborated
Hypothesized
Verified
Unknown
```

Unknown is a valid research state.

---

## Principle 19 — Causal Claims Require Additional Evidence

A change observed after an intervention does not automatically prove that the intervention caused the change.

```text
Intervention
     ↓
Observed Change
     ≠
Proven Causation
```

Causal claims require appropriate experimental evidence and consideration of alternative explanations.

---

## Principle 20 — Research Should Be Reproducible

Whenever practical, another researcher should be able to understand:

* what entity was evaluated
* which system was evaluated
* which query or prompt was used
* under which conditions
* what intervention occurred
* how the result was measured
* what evidence supported the conclusion

---

## Principle 21 — Research Should Be Responsible

AIO CODE research should use legitimate, documented, and proportionate interventions.

The project must not intentionally rely on:

* deception
* fraud
* unauthorized access
* abuse of systems
* fabricated identities
* fabricated evidence
* manipulation intended to conceal research activity or findings

---

## Principle 22 — Findings Must Be Proportional to Evidence

Research communication should distinguish among:

```text
What we observed
What we corroborated
What we verified
What we interpret
What we hypothesize
What remains unknown
```

This distinction must remain visible in technical documentation, datasets, publications, and public explanations.

---

## Core AIO CODE Research Rule

The central rule of AIO CODE is:

> **Evidence determines the strength of the claim.**

Supporting principles:

> **Identity should be established before visibility is optimized.**

> **Measure before claiming improvement.**

> **A measured change does not automatically establish causation.**

> **Unknown is a valid research result.**
